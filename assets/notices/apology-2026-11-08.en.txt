Hello, I am Violet, the developer of AsoBMaShow. I apologize to HorieYuuka, who was harmed by AsoBMaShow’s unauthorized use of their Be-Music Script package search/download API, and to the BMS community, who were also affected by the server shutdown measures resulting from my wrongdoing.

# What happened
On June 14, 2026, I integrated HorieYuuka’s service without their permission into AsoBMaShow, which I was developing for personal use, so that I could conveniently download BMS files for myself. Because I integrated it without asking whether an external app was permitted to use that REST API, even my personal use was not justified. I also failed to correct this problem before releasing the app publicly. As a result, the REST API integration was included in the first public version on October 3, 2026. In my immaturity, I was preoccupied with adding features and did not look back at my past mistakes, leaving this implementation in place until November 1, 2026. I fully recognize my responsibility for integrating the feature without permission and for failing to correct it after the public release.

On October 24, 2026, HorieYuuka identified improper use of the REST API, temporarily shut down the server, and introduced measures including Cloudflare Turnstile and User Agent filtering to block automatic downloads through the REST API. Irresponsibly, I did not discover that access to the REST API had been restricted until November 1, 2026. There was also an implementation problem: when searching for BMS files through HorieYuuka’s service, the app could retry with other queries instead of correctly handling access denials. I am also responsible for releasing this behavior without carefully reviewing it.

On November 1, 2026, I developed a feature for AsoBMaShow that recognized BMS package files downloaded through an in-app browser and automatically added them to the library. While testing this feature and the existing download UI, which I was redesigning at the same time, I became aware that HorieYuuka’s REST API had been blocked. I should have removed the integration with HorieYuuka’s service entirely at that point. Instead, with the careless thought that “perhaps it would be acceptable if users downloaded files themselves through the in-app browser,” I added HorieYuuka’s webpage to the browser’s default bookmarks. Once I became aware of the restriction, I should have checked HorieYuuka’s service usage policy again. My mistake was to assume on my own that manual downloads through a browser would be acceptable without doing so.

Later, while testing the in-app browser’s download feature on various sites, I noticed that only the download button on HorieYuuka’s site did not respond, and incorrectly concluded that this was a browser compatibility issue. At the time, I did not realize that HorieYuuka had added restrictions specifically targeting AsoBMaShow. I mistakenly believed that downloads were not working because the app did not use ordinary browser behavior and identifying information (User Agent). I was wrong to fail to ask about HorieYuuka’s position even at this stage.

Under that mistaken belief, I removed the AsoBMaShow identifier from the iOS User Agent to change it to one used by an ordinary browser, and enabled the browser capabilities needed for Cloudflare Turnstile’s human verification logic to work correctly. I subsequently changed the Android User Agent in the same way, removing its WebView identifier. During this process, I completely removed the integration with HorieYuuka’s REST API.

I am responsible both for assuming on my own, despite being aware of the restriction and without asking about HorieYuuka’s position, that changing the download method to visiting the site and downloading manually would be acceptable, and for consequently distributing the app in a form that bypassed HorieYuuka’s blocking policy.
The in-app browser feature was released on November 2, 2026. On November 7, 2026, I belatedly learned that HorieYuuka did not want an in-app browser integration either. I immediately removed their site from the in-app browser’s bookmarks and released the change.

# Why the apology was delayed
HorieYuuka posted a warning about unauthorized integration without directly naming AsoBMaShow on October 24, 2026, and identified AsoBMaShow as the app responsible and raised the issue on November 2, 2026. They also sent me a DM on X on November 2, 2026. However, I had disabled notifications for DMs from users I did not follow, and I failed to see HorieYuuka’s posts on X or their post in DC Inside’s rhythm game gallery. I only became aware of them on November 7, 2026, after an acquaintance informed me. As the developer of publicly available software, I was responsible for regularly checking official contact channels and message requests. My failure to do so was entirely my fault.

However, discovering these messages late in no way justifies my failure to contact HorieYuuka after recognizing the API block on November 1.

As soon as I became aware of HorieYuuka’s messages on November 7, 2026, I privately explained how the issue had arisen and apologized. Even in that explanation, however, I stated some facts incorrectly. When explaining the circumstances privately, I said that the User Agent for HorieYuuka’s REST API appeared to have been changed as well while I was using Codex to fix a bug related to the GingerRush integration. I had not carefully checked the records and presented mere speculation as though I had verified the facts. After carefully retracing my memory and reviewing the relevant change history, I confirmed that the GingerRush bug-fix commit did not include any User Agent change for the REST API, and that what had actually changed was the in-app browser’s User Agent. Accordingly, on November 8, 2026, I wrote this apology, correcting my inaccurate account of the facts and providing additional details. With HorieYuuka’s approval to publish this apology, I am making it publicly available on November 9, 2026.

During this review, I also reexamined how the app integrates with other external services. For GingerRush, Konmai, and Wriggle, AsoBMaShow downloads files by song hash through APIs used in other open-source BMS clients. Although I implemented these integrations by referring to those clients, the publicly available materials I have checked so far do not establish whether these APIs are available for everyone to use or are permitted only for those particular open-source clients. Whether AsoBMaShow is permitted to use them therefore remains unclear. BMS Search provides information about a public API that external apps can use, but AsoBMaShow was extracting download links directly from HTML pages instead of using that API. I have not confirmed whether BMS Search permits this HTML collection method. The Bokutachi IR integration uses a documented public API, and I confirmed that the official documentation explains how external programs can submit scores to the IR and retrieve rankings.

# What I will do from now on
First, so that as many AsoBMaShow users as possible can learn what happened and understand my wrongdoing and responsibility as its developer, I will make this post accessible from a clearly visible place on the app’s start screen.

To prevent similar problems from happening again, I will carefully review the permitted scope of use whenever adding an external service integration or expanding an existing one, and record the evidence supporting it. If access to an existing integration is restricted even after permission has been confirmed, I will immediately suspend the integration and decide whether to resume it only after reconfirming the service operator’s position, including any changes in the permitted scope. I will never continue using a service by changing the User Agent or the supported method of access before reconfirming permission.

As a starting point, since the permitted scope of use for GingerRush, Konmai, Wriggle, and BMS Search is currently unclear, I will first remove BMS downloads and the in-app browser, carry out a thorough review, and then decide whether to resume their use.

When I contacted HorieYuuka about compensation for the harm caused, they said they did not want financial compensation. Instead, they requested an explanation of how I came to use the service without permission and why I continued supporting the integration through another method after access was restricted, along with a public acknowledgment of my responsibility. Accordingly, I will carry out the measures described above and share my progress.

Once again, I apologize to HorieYuuka for integrating their service into the app without permission, for continuing to support the integration by changing the method based on my own assumptions about what was allowed even after I became aware of the access restriction, and for incorrectly explaining the facts in my earlier private apology. I also offer my sincere apology to the BMS community, who were using the service legitimately but experienced inconvenience because of me.

November 10 update: After I contacted Catizard, the operator of GingerRush, they granted permission to use the download API. I have therefore restored downloads through GingerRush.
