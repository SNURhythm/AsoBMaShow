# Completion sound direction, revision 2

The first audition was rejected as generic. An artifact-clean render is not a
successful design. This revision uses Earshot's CLAP-backed critic, calibrated
sound descriptions, explicit confusion alternatives and independent AudioSet
tags to identify what the sounds actually suggest. Model scores guide revisions;
they do not override the user's listening judgement.

## Shared language: kinetic glass and brushed metal

The object should feel designed and tangible: contact, body, a small cloud of
fine detail, then space. A firmly defined midrange strike makes the sound legible
on small speakers. Dense, slightly inharmonic upper partials make the material
feel glassy rather than a sine-wave doorbell. A changing spectral envelope opens
at the strike and darkens as it decays. High-frequency noise is a brief silky
shear, not a constant white-noise wash. Low weight stays short and mostly central;
upper detail spreads into a diffuse stereo tail. No three-note melody, casino
arpeggio, laser zap, repeating shimmer loop, or gratuitous second impact.

The three outcomes belong to the same material family. Their hierarchy comes
from the size of the excitation and the depth of the resonance, not from playing
the same sound louder. Silence after the gesture is part of the design.

### Full Combo — precise release

A close, crisp brushed-alloy contact releases a small bright glass resonance.
A quick, thin air movement suggests the seven lime trails rising; the body is
compact and dry enough to feel immediate. A little friction at the attack keeps
it tactile, with a short softened ring behind it. It should read as an earned,
clean completion rather than a message arriving. The principal contact is at
80 ms. Most energy clears within roughly 450 ms, with a quiet tail below 1.2 s.

### Perfect — a single luminous bloom

One substantial but controlled glass/metal strike at 80 ms, with a rounded
low-mid body and a rich upper resonance that opens smoothly after contact.
The gold pulse expands once. A diffuse, silk-like halo follows that expansion,
with no extra bell notes or second transient. The bright edge loses high
frequencies quickly; a warm, spacious afterimage remains. More depth and width
than Full Combo, but no dramatic bass slam. Audible decay around 1.3–1.6 s.

### MAX — concentrated energy released

A brief, restrained suction of textured air accompanies the 140 ms gathering
strokes. At the burst, one tight, full-bodied impact blooms into a dense
crystalline resonance. A small amount of low-frequency pressure gives the star
weight; a textured midrange strike must still carry it on a phone speaker.
Fine, irregular high-frequency particles disperse outward with the shards.
They fuse into one decaying gesture rather than a sequence of plinks. The halo
widens after the centered impact, then loses brightness and level together.
No explosion, trailer braam, slot-machine fanfare or long synth riser. Audible
decay around 1.6–2.0 s; the MAX title holds after the sound clears.

## Feedback constraints

- Describe the rejected baseline before revising; keep it for A/B comparison.
- Score matching short acoustic descriptions against phone notifications,
  bell melodies, cartoon game bleeps, laser effects and explosions as relevant.
- Examine unrestricted top descriptions and independent AudioSet labels as well
  as the hand-written target. A higher target score alone is insufficient.
- Use designed mode: this is intentionally designed audio, so a real-recording
  classifier is diagnostic, not the objective.
- Preserve the fixed visual accent times through candidate generation.
- Revise material, envelope or balance in response to specific feedback.
- Deliver another synchronized audition. Keep all revisions local; do not push
  until approved sound assets are actually integrated into gameplay.
